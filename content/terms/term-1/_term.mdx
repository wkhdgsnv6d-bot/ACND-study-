Term 1 is deliberately narrow. There are a dozen services an agency like Ascend
could eventually sell, and trying to learn all of them at once is the most
reliable way to be mediocre at every one. So this term concentrates on three
technical disciplines and refuses to be distracted:

1. **Website development** — the thing clients can see, and usually the first
   thing they buy.
2. **CRM** — where a business's customer relationships actually live, and the
   system everything else has to integrate with.
3. **AI automation** — the work that produces recurring revenue rather than
   one-off projects.

SEO, AI customer service and Voice AI are real services and you will learn them,
but not here. They are Term 2 and Term 3 material. Adding them now would give
you four half-skills instead of three whole ones.

## The fourth discipline

Alongside those three sits something less glamorous and more important: **risk
and reliability**. The gap between a hobbyist automation and a professional one
is almost never cleverness. It is what happens when the API is down, the webhook
fires twice, the model returns nonsense, or the client's ops manager pastes
something strange into a form.

Every system you build in this term needs a written failure plan. That is not
bureaucracy — it is the difference between a client system you can sleep through
and one that wakes you up.

## Talking to businesses from week one

Module 2 asks you to conduct five market-validation conversations with real
businesses. Not at the end of the term. Early, while you are still learning to
build.

The reason is simple: it is entirely possible to spend six months becoming a
competent developer and discover you have built skills nobody nearby wants to
buy. Discovering that in week three costs you a few awkward conversations.
Discovering it in month six costs you six months.

## What "client ready" means here

The Term 1 milestone is **Ascend Technical Client Ready**. It is not awarded for
finishing the lessons. It requires demonstrated competence in website
development, CRM, AI automation, risk and reliability, testing, deployment and
documentation — plus evidence that you have spoken to real businesses.

That is a deliberately high bar, because the alternative is taking money from
someone for a system you cannot actually support.
